About YouthLink Uganda

A student project built to reduce the information gap that keeps many young Ugandans from finding work and skills opportunities.

Our mission

Every year hundreds of thousands of young people enter the Ugandan labour market, yet many struggle to discover the limited opportunities that do exist. Information is scattered across WhatsApp groups, social media and hard-to-navigate portals. YouthLink Uganda brings jobs, skills programmes, practical guidance and mentorship connections into one simple, mobile-friendly website.

The site was planned and built by a group of twelve students as part of the Internet Technology & Web Design module. It is intentionally realistic in scope so that it can be maintained and improved after the course ends.

What you will find here

  • Jobs & Internships _ Entry-level vacancies and practical placements, filterable by location and type.
  • Skills & Training _ Free and low-cost programmes that improve employability.
  • Career Guidance _ CV tips, interview advice and sector overviews written for the local context.
  • Mentors & Stories _ Real examples of progress and people willing to share advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is the website free to use?

Yes. Browsing jobs, skills and guidance is free for everyone.

How do I post a job or internship?

Send the details (title, location, requirements, how to apply, deadline) via the contact form or email. Listings are reviewed before they appear.

Are the jobs and programmes real?

The current content is sample data created for this student project. In a production version, only verified opportunities would be published. Always confirm details with the organisation before applying.

Who built this site?

A group of twelve students from the Bachelor of Science in Information Technology programme, Victoria university, as part of the Internet Technology & Web Design module (Group Project 1 & 2, 2026).

Can I reuse the design or code?

The code is written for educational purposes. You may study and adapt it for learning, with attribution.

Sources & acknowledgements

The problem definition and focus of this website draw on publicly reported labour-market data, including:

  • Uganda Bureau of Statistics (UBOS) Labour Market Survey findings (2025 dissemination)
  • Contemporary news reporting (Nile Post, Red Pepper, Ecofin Agency)
  • Afrobarometer Dispatch AD975 on youth outlook in Uganda

All sample job and programme content is fictional and created for demonstration only.
