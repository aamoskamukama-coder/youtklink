Career Guidance

Practical, locally relevant advice to help you present yourself well and search for work more effectively.

CV & Cover Letter Tips

A good CV for most entry-level roles in Uganda is 1-2 pages, clear, and focused on what you can offer.

  • Contact details - Name, phone, professional email, and (optional) LinkedIn or portfolio link.
  • Profile / objective - 2-3 lines stating the kind of role you want and your strongest relevant strength.
  • Education - Most recent first. Include relevant projects or coursework.
  • Experience - Include internships, volunteer work, campus leadership and part-time jobs. Use action verbs and results where possible.
  • Skills - List tools and soft skills that match the job advert. Be honest.
  • References - “Available on request” is usually enough at application stage.

Save your CV as a PDF with a clear filename (e.g. Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf). Keep the design simple so it opens cleanly on any device.

Interview Preparation

Most interviews for junior roles explore three areas: your motivation, your basic skills, and whether you will fit the team.

  • Research the organisation - what they do and any recent news.
  • Prepare short examples of times you solved a problem, worked in a team, or learned something quickly.
  • Dress neatly and arrive early (or test your internet and camera for online interviews).
  • Prepare 1-2 questions to ask them (about the role, training, or next steps).
  • After the interview, send a short thank-you message if you have an email contact.

Sector overviews (quick guides)

Understanding a sector helps you target the right opportunities and skills:

  • ICT / Tech - Junior developer, support, data entry, digital marketing. Portfolio and practical skills often matter more than the exact degree.
  • Agriculture & agribusiness - Extension, input sales, record-keeping, digital platforms for farmers. Field experience is valued.
  • Health support roles - Data, community mobilisation, logistics. Reliability and accuracy are essential.
  • Education - Teaching assistants, after-school programmes, education NGOs. Patience and clear communication help.
  • Creative & media - Content, design, social media. A small portfolio of real work (even for campus or community projects) stands out.

Combine these overviews with the Skills and Jobs sections to plan your next step.
